**A high-performance research platform for imperfect-information
decision-making, centered on heads-up no-limit Hold'em.**

PokerAlpha solves imperfect-information games with CFR / CFR+ /
external-sampling MCCFR, validates every solver against exactly solvable
reduced games, trains large abstract Hold'em strategies on a C++17 native
backend that is a measured **38× faster** than the Python reference (proven
bitwise-equivalent), and — the part most systems skip — **quantifies when
its own strategy should not be trusted**: a confidence/abstention layer
calibrated against exact-game EV regret decides per decision whether to use
the solver, fall back, or abstain. Every number below traces to a committed
result file; the limitations are measured, not disclaimed.

## Why this is hard

- **Imperfect information**: strategies must be fixed per *information set*,
  and equilibrium play requires mixing — there is no single "correct move"
  to supervise against.
- **Scale**: heads-up no-limit Hold'em has ~10¹⁶⁰ states; everything runs
  through an abstraction whose error must itself be measured.
- **Imperfect recall**: the tractable abstraction deliberately forgets
  history, which voids CFR's equilibrium guarantee — so correctness
  evidence has to come from exactly solvable reduced games instead.
- **Stochastic training**: external-sampling MCCFR converges in
  expectation; independently seeded runs disagree, and the hard question is
  whether that disagreement is harmless mixing between near-equal actions
  or genuine EV uncertainty. This project measures the difference.
- **Two-language equivalence**: a 38× C++ port is only useful if it is the
  *same algorithm* — down to IEEE evaluation order (clang's FMA contraction
  broke bit parity in the last ulp and had to be disabled).
- **Trust under abstraction**: a confident-looking policy table says nothing
  about reliability; deciding *when to abstain* is its own calibrated,
  falsifiable sub-problem.

## Native MCCFR: 38× faster

![Python vs native MCCFR throughput](results/figures/native_speedup.png)

*Generated by `python experiments/make_readme_figures.py` from
[`results/benchmarks/native_mccfr_v1.json`](results/benchmarks/native_mccfr_v1.json).*

Profiling put ~60% of Python training time in hand evaluation and river
percentile tables; the fix was architectural, not micro: the **entire hot
path moved to C++17** — game rules, dealing, evaluator, information-set
encoder, regret matching, sampling, and the recursive traversal itself.
Python crosses the pybind11 boundary **once per training chunk** (GIL
released); there are no per-node callbacks. Infosets are packed 64-bit keys
over fixed-size native nodes.

Correctness was protected, not assumed: the two backends are **bitwise
identical** through 1,000 iterations on a shared random tape (trajectories,
regret sums, average strategies); the evaluator is pinned on all 2,598,960
five-card hands; 20k+ random reachable states match on rules, legal actions
and encoder keys; ASan/UBSan clean. `-ffast-math` was never enabled, and
`-ffp-contract=off` is required — FMA fusion changed betting arithmetic in
the last ulp and the parity suite caught it. Checkpoints are versioned,
checksummed, and resume exactly (`train(a)+save+load+train(b)` is
bit-identical to `train(a+b)`).

[design](docs/native_solver_design.md) ·
[benchmark](docs/native_solver_benchmark.md).

## Solver trust: calibrated abstention

PokerAlpha does not blindly trust its trained policy. Three independently
seeded runs expose training uncertainty — but measured action EVs show most
policy disagreement is **harmless mixing between near-equal actions**
(~94% of preflop first-action disagreement has no significant EV
consequence). So confidence is calibrated against what matters:

![Risk-coverage: v1 vs v2 confidence](results/figures/confidence_risk_coverage.png)

*Generated by `python experiments/make_readme_figures.py` from
[`results/validation/confidence_signal_quality.json`](results/validation/confidence_signal_quality.json).*

- Nine exactly solved reduced games provide **true per-infoset EV regret**
  for 8,880 training snapshots; every confidence signal is scored against
  it with leave-one-game-out evaluation.
- Finding: checkpoint *movement* predicts EV regret at **no horizon**;
  visit count (AUC 0.74) and cross-seed disagreement are the real signals.
  The v1 gate's movement baseline penalized mature strategies for distance
  traveled — v2 fixes exactly that one definition and nothing else,
  yielding **1.7× the coverage at equal accepted risk** (held-out).
- At decision time the gate returns accept / low-confidence / reject with
  machine-readable reasons; rejected lookups fall back to rollouts, then
  heuristics. Streets with measured abstraction error (flop, turn) are
  permanently capped at low confidence — stable training never erases
  known abstraction risk.
- Confidence is a calibrated accept/abstain rule on measured proxies — it
  is *not* a probability that the solver is correct.

## The original research: adaptive play under an exploitability budget

The project began as a quantitative study on Kuhn/Leduc poker, where exact
EV and exploitability are computable:

> **How much equilibrium robustness should an agent sacrifice to exploit
> statistically detected opponent behavior, under estimation error,
> distribution shift, and limited computation?**

Key measured findings (full writeup: **[RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)**;
reference detail: [docs/research_details.md](docs/research_details.md)):

1. **Exploitation pays, bounded**: vs a "maniac", equilibrium earns −9.0
   chips/100, an oracle best response +77.8 (at ~1,000× the
   exploitability); the budget-capped adaptive agent recovers +10.9 at
   ε ≤ 0.1.
2. **Stationary Bayesian inference fails permanently under regime change**
   — the posterior stays >0.99 confident on the *wrong* opponent forever;
   exponential forgetting repairs recovery (380–500 hands) at a measured
   2× false-switch cost.
3. **The best solver depends on the compute budget**: CFR beats CFR+ below
   ~0.5 s; they cross near 1 s; CFR+ is 19× better by 20 s.
4. **Profile-driven evaluator optimization: 11.0×** (37.7k → 415k evals/s),
   proven bit-identical — with untouched workloads as the built-in noise
   control.

## Hold'em decision-support platform

One normalized path from any input — screenshot, hand history, simulator,
manual entry — through ranges, the gated solver, rollouts and heuristics to
a `DecisionReport` with EV ± SE, provenance and warnings.

| area | summary | docs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engine | 2–9 seats, blinds/antes, min-raise and short-all-in reopening, side pots | [holdem_engine](docs/holdem_engine.md) |
| Solver | external-sampling MCCFR (Python reference + native backend) on a locked v2 config: compact imperfect-recall encoder, exact river-percentile buckets, legal NLHE sizing | [abstraction](docs/abstraction.md) |
| Ranges / opponent | 1,326-combo weighted ranges, Bayesian updates, multiway equity, statistics with credible intervals | [ranges](docs/ranges.md) |
| Decision engine | solver → rollout → heuristic with coded rejection reasons and per-source uncertainty | [decision_engine](docs/decision_engine.md) |
| Observer | optional, read-only screenshot reader with calibration, template OCR, fusion, test-session recorder | [observer](docs/observer.md) |
| UI | `streamlit run poker_alpha/ui/app.py` — display only | [architecture](docs/architecture.md) |

What the platform does **not** claim: a solved game, GTO play, a profitable
bot, Nash guarantees for 3+ players, ground-truth opponent ranges, or
validated real-screen recognition. Uncertainty is carried, shown, and
gated — not hidden.

## Limitations

Measured where possible; all bound the claims above.

- **Imperfect-recall abstraction** — no equilibrium guarantee exists for
  the trained Hold'em strategy by construction.
- **Full-game exploitability is bounded below, not computed**: the
  restricted-LBR floor of ~100 bb/100 applies to every strategy in this
  family; exact best response on the full game is infeasible
  ([feasibility study](results/validation/best_response_feasibility.json)).
- **Flop/turn abstraction error is real and measured** (3.7 / 5.1 bb median
  on exact subgames) — those streets are capped at low confidence
  ([summary](docs/abstraction_error_summary.md)).
- **A small consequential uncertainty cluster remains**: jam/3-bet-defense
  spots with medium pairs still carry cross-seed EV uncertainty (up to
  ~15 bb) at 2M iterations; they stay gated.
- **Rollout fallback is heuristic**: 20/36 agreement with exact river
  equilibria, documented over-bet bias, opponent-model sensitivity
  ([audit](results/validation/final_rollout_audit.json)).
- **Multiplayer advice is approximate** — CFR's two-player zero-sum
  guarantees do not extend to 3–9 players.
- **Opponent archetypes in the original research are synthetic**, and some
  realized-EV comparisons have wide CIs (the exact-EV experiments are the
  load-bearing evidence).
- **Real-screen observer accuracy is not independently validated** (one
  tuning frame, zero held-out frames).
- **No claim of real-money profitability.** This is a research system.

Historical phase-by-phase limitations are preserved in
[RESEARCH.md §12](RESEARCH.md), [docs/validation.md](docs/validation.md)
and the docs above — the list here reflects the current system.

## Safety / intended use

The optional screen observer **reads pixels only**: nothing in PokerAlpha
clicks, types, controls a browser, or submits actions. Real-time assistance
is only for private, play-money or test games where it is permitted —
elsewhere, use the same pipeline for post-hand and post-session analysis.
No claim is made about profitability in real-money poker.

## Design principles

- Solvers depend only on a minimal `Game` interface; one implementation
  trains on every game in the project.
- All reported numbers come from committed, regenerable runs.
- Results carry uncertainty wherever sampling is involved; exact
  computation is preferred wherever the game is small enough.
- Negative results are reported, not buried — the regime-change failure,
  the MCCFR-on-small-games non-result, and the ~100 bb/100 exploitability
  floor are all load-bearing parts of the writeup.
